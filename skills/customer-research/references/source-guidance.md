# Source Guidance

Use this reference when deciding how much weight to give each source.

## Authority weighting

1. Direct user-provided scope: strongest for what the user wants reviewed.
2. Direct customer communications: strongest for commitments, objections, approvals, and next steps.
3. Calendar: reliable for scheduled meetings and attendees, weak for outcomes.
4. Proposal, estimate, or contract docs: strong for scope and pricing position, but check freshness.
5. Project/task tools: strong for delivery state, weak for sales intent.
6. Support systems: strong for service risk and customer pain, not a proxy for commercial value.
7. Internal chat: useful context, but informal and often incomplete.
8. Public web and LinkedIn: useful for current external context, but not proof of private intent.

## Common overreach to avoid

- Inferring buyer authority from job title alone.
- Treating a website redesign need as budget confirmation.
- Treating a reply delay as disinterest without supporting context.
- Treating internal enthusiasm as customer urgency.
- Treating an old proposal as active without recent confirmation.
- Treating no support tickets as proof of satisfaction.

## Safe inference wording

Use phrases such as:

- `this suggests, but does not prove`
- `the safest read is`
- `evidence supports asking about`
- `current evidence is not enough to claim`
- `before pushing commercially, confirm`
