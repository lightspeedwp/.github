Treat these as red flags even if the overall readiness score appears strong.

## Governance red flags

- No clear governance owner.
- No content approver.
- No approval workflow for AI-generated content.
- No incident contact for incorrect or risky outputs.
- Staff use public AI tools without data rules.
- No process for correcting inaccurate AI outputs.

## Content red flags

- No approved source-of-truth documents.
- Service, product, pricing or policy content is inconsistent.
- Important pages are thin, duplicated or outdated.
- Claims, statistics or credentials have no supporting evidence.
- FAQs are missing or based on memory rather than approved answers.

## Privacy and compliance red flags

- Privacy, cookie or terms documents are missing or outdated.
- Forms collect personal data without clear ownership or purpose.
- Chatbot lead capture is planned without data boundaries.
- Sensitive, financial, health, children, legal, employment or regulated data is involved.
- Conversation logs are planned but retention and access controls are not defined.

## Chatbot red flags

- The client wants a chatbot before content governance exists.
- No approved source list exists.
- No exclusion list exists.
- No human handoff route exists.
- The chatbot is expected to answer everything.
- The chatbot would need to make up answers due to missing FAQs or policies.
- No transcript review owner is named.

## Technical red flags

- Website is slow, fragile or difficult to maintain.
- Plugins/themes/core are not maintained.
- No backup, staging or update process exists.
- Forms, analytics or CRM routes are undocumented.
- Content editors rely on one-off layouts rather than reusable blocks/components.

## Response behaviour

When a red flag is detected:

1. Name it clearly.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Ask the most relevant follow-up questions.
4. Recommend foundation or governance work before implementation.
5. Avoid speculative or overconfident recommendations.
