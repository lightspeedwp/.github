Use these as reference points when producing AI readiness, governance, content collection or chatbot planning outputs. Do not over-cite in client documents unless the client needs a formal governance pack.

## AI governance and risk

| Framework | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| NIST AI Risk Management Framework | Useful for mapping, measuring, managing and governing AI risks across practical workflows. |
| NIST Generative AI Profile | Helpful for generative AI-specific risks such as hallucinations, misuse, data leakage and unsafe outputs. |
| ISO/IEC 42001 | Useful for management-system thinking, ownership, review cycles and operational controls. |
| EU AI Act | Important for risk-based thinking and transparency where users interact with AI systems. |
| UK ICO AI and data protection guidance | Useful for privacy, lawful processing, minimisation and human review considerations. |
| South African POPIA | Relevant where South African clients, customers or operations process personal information. |
| OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications | Useful for prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, insecure output handling, vector weaknesses and excessive agency. |

## Website quality and content

| Framework | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| WCAG 2.2 | Accessibility baseline for website and chatbot experience design. |
| W3C evaluation guidance | Reinforces that automated checks are useful but do not replace manual accessibility testing. |
| Google Search Central guidance on generative AI content | AI-assisted content must be useful, original, accurate and not low-value scaled spam. |
| Core Web Vitals | Practical user-experience and performance signals for website readiness. |
| Structured data guidance | Helps search engines and AI systems understand entities, page types and content relationships. |
| WordPress performance/security/content governance best practice | Supports maintainable WordPress builds, safer editing and more reliable publishing workflows. |
