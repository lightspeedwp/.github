# Output Standards

## Default structure

Start substantial outputs with:

- **Value** — what the recommendation helps LightSpeed decide or deliver.
- **Risks** — missing evidence, assumptions, approvals, implementation constraints, or commercial uncertainty.
- **Next step** — the most useful next action.

Then provide numbered actions, a workstream table, or a concise routing report.

## Required sections when relevant

- Client or project summary
- Evidence reviewed
- Confirmed facts
- Assumptions
- Open questions
- Risks and blockers
- Recommended route or package path
- Specialist routing table
- Internal LightSpeed notes
- Client-facing summary, only when requested

## Evidence labels

Use these labels when evidence quality matters:

- Confirmed
- Assumed
- Needs confirmation
- Source gap
- Risky or approval-dependent
- Legal/privacy/security review required

## Commercial posture

- Choose the base route before add-ons.
- State whether the recommendation is provisional, audit-first, fixed-fee eligible, approval-dependent, or custom-scope.
- Do not present pricing or timelines as final unless they are explicitly supplied or approved.
- For internal work, use complexity/risk unless hours are requested.
- For client work, use hours or effort bands only when appropriate and clearly caveated.

## Client-ready material

When drafting client-facing content:

- Keep internal reasoning out of the client section.
- Do not include unapproved claims, private notes, or uncertain pricing.
- Use plain UK English.
- Add legal/privacy/security review notes where needed rather than pretending the wording is final.
