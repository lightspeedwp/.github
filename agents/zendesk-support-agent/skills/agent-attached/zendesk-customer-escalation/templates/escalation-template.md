## Summary

State the issue in 1-3 sentences. Include the Zendesk ticket reference, customer/account, affected product or workflow, and why this needs escalation rather than routine support handling.

## Customer impact

Describe who is affected, what they cannot do, how often it occurs, business or relationship pressure, and any known deadlines, SLA risks, launch dates, renewal pressure, or executive visibility.

## Severity and urgency

Use one of: `Critical`, `High`, or `Medium`.

Explain the evidence behind the rating. Do not rate based only on customer tone. If business context is missing, say so.

## Decision or action needed

State the exact ask for the receiving team. Phrase it as a decision, investigation, confirmation, ownership request, or remediation action.

## Recommended owner

Name the best escalation lane: Engineering, Product, Security, Leadership, or Support specialist. Add a named team or owner only when the evidence clearly supports it.

## Evidence gathered

List the strongest evidence from Zendesk and approved shared sources. Include timestamps, symptoms, error wording, ticket notes, screenshots, logs, similar tickets, known-issue links, or policy references when available.

## Sources checked / unavailable

For shared-agent runs, state which sources were checked and which were unavailable, access denied, not found, or not applicable. Distinguish missing access from a confirmed lack of evidence. Omit this section only when source availability is obvious and not relevant to confidence.

## Attempted steps

Summarise what support has already tried and the result of each step. Include configuration checks, reproduction attempts, customer guidance, workarounds, or prior internal reviews.

## Reproduction / diagnostic notes

For bug-like issues, include environment, account/site/user, versions, integrations, affected URL or object, expected behaviour, actual behaviour, and reproduction steps where known.

## Evidence gaps / open questions

List only gaps that materially affect confidence, urgency, ownership, or the requested decision. Do not hide uncertainty.

## Suggested internal checkpoint

State when the escalation should be reviewed if no owner response or decision arrives. Use a concrete checkpoint such as the next business day, before the next customer update, or before a named deadline.

## Customer-facing commitment / next update

Record what the customer has already been told and what support should say next. If no customer-facing update has been promised, say so.
