Use these examples to replace unsafe or overconfident support wording. Do not add facts that are not present in the supplied evidence.

## Unsupported fix promise

Avoid:

> We will have this fixed by tomorrow.

Use:

> We are investigating this and will update you once we have a confirmed next step.

## Unsupported root cause

Avoid:

> This was caused by the recent release.

Use:

> We have not confirmed the cause yet. The timing overlaps with the recent release, so that is one area to verify.

## Unsupported engineering commitment

Avoid:

> Engineering will prioritise this immediately.

Use:

> We can escalate this with the evidence collected so the relevant team can assess priority.

## Refund, credit, or billing risk

Avoid:

> We will apply a credit for this issue.

Use:

> I will check what options are available under our support and billing process.

## Policy exception risk

Avoid:

> We can make an exception in this case.

Use:

> I will check whether there is an approved path for this request and come back with a confirmed answer.

## SLA overstatement

Avoid:

> This is outside SLA.

Use:

> I do not have enough evidence here to confirm the SLA position. Verify the ticket timestamps and applicable support terms before making this statement.

## Customer impact overstatement

Avoid:

> This affected all customers.

Use:

> The supplied evidence confirms impact for the customers or tickets listed here. Wider impact still needs verification.

## Data or security sensitivity

Avoid:

> No data was exposed.

Use:

> The supplied evidence does not confirm data exposure, but a security-sensitive claim should be verified through the approved security review path before sharing externally.

## Knowledge-base stability

Avoid:

> Customers should use this workaround.

Use:

> This workaround appears to be case-specific based on the supplied evidence. Verify that it is stable and reusable before publishing it as public guidance.

## Delay apology without blame

Use:

> Thanks for your patience while we check this. I do not want to overstate the cause before we have confirmed it, so the next step is to verify the evidence and come back with a clear update.
