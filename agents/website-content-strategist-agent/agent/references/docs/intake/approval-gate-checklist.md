## Purpose

This file is the current-state final approval boundary for intake-heavy workflows.

Use it to decide whether an output is:

- only suitable for planning
- ready for structured review
- ready for approval routing
- actually approved for use
- blocked from approval because material gates are still open

It is the final decision layer after source review, evidence extraction, exclusions, claim handling, risk review, and blocking-gap checks.

---

## Approval outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| planning_ready | The work is useful for planning or scoping, but not ready to be treated as approved content or approved reusable truth. | Keep it in planning or intake mode. |
| review_ready | The work is structured enough for formal review, but approval has not happened yet. | Route to the right reviewer or owner. |
| approval_ready | The work has the right structure, evidence posture, and review packaging for a decision by the approval owner. | Present it for sign-off. |
| approved_for_use | The work is approved within its stated scope and can be used accordingly. | Use within the approved boundary only. |
| blocked_from_approval | Material gaps or risks still prevent approval. | Do not treat the output as ready for sign-off. |

---

## Core rule

Do not let a polished output look approved just because it is well structured.

An output is not approved-for-use unless:

- the right owner or authority is identified
- the relevant sources are approved
- claim-sensitive content has been reviewed where needed
- exclusions and scope boundaries are respected
- material blockers are surfaced
- any approval-dependent wording is clearly flagged if still pending

---

## Approval gate checklist

### 1. Scope and purpose gate

- [ ] The output purpose is clear.
- [ ] The output is correctly framed as planning, review, approval, or approved-use material.
- [ ] The scope is specific enough that approval means something real.
- [ ] The output does not silently widen scope beyond what is intended.

If any of these fail, the output is at most **planning_ready** or **blocked_from_approval**.

### 2. Source authority gate

- [ ] The key sources are identified.
- [ ] Source approval status is known where it materially matters.
- [ ] Reference-only sources are not being treated as approved truth.
- [ ] Public URLs, questionnaires, screenshots, rough notes, or ambiguous sources are not being treated as approval authorities by default.

If source authority is unclear for a material decision, the output is not **approval_ready**.

### 3. Evidence gate

- [ ] Important factual statements have supporting evidence status.
- [ ] Evidence is clearly marked as confirmed, inferred, defaulted, missing, or unsupported.
- [ ] Weak evidence is still visible rather than hidden by polished wording.
- [ ] Evidence-sensitive outputs distinguish between approved truth and working assumptions.

If evidence posture is weak but usable for planning, use **planning_ready** or **review_ready**.
If evidence posture is too weak for sign-off, use **blocked_from_approval**.

### 4. Claim gate

- [ ] Claim-sensitive content has been identified.
- [ ] Claim-bearing items are either approved, clearly pending, or blocked.
- [ ] The claim register has been used when needed.
- [ ] Unsafe or unsupported claims are not present as if approved.
- [ ] Safer rewrites are noted when claims cannot be approved as written.

If claim-sensitive content is unresolved, the output cannot be **approved_for_use**.

### 5. Exclusions and boundary gate

- [ ] Confirmed exclusions are respected.
- [ ] Must-not-do rules are reflected in the output.
- [ ] Unsupported areas are not being presented as allowed.
- [ ] The output does not conflict with stronger approved authority.

If exclusions are still unresolved in a material way, use **review_ready** or **blocked_from_approval** depending on severity.

### 6. Sensitive-content gate

- [ ] Legal, privacy, medical, financial, safety, compliance, or regulated issues have been identified if present.
- [ ] Sensitive wording is not being presented as certain when review is missing.
- [ ] Governance-sensitive outputs include the right review path or escalation notes.
- [ ] Policy-style wording is not being treated as final legal certainty without the right review.

If sensitive content is present without the required review, use **blocked_from_approval**.

### 7. Approval-owner gate

- [ ] The approval owner is known when approval is needed.
- [ ] The reviewer, approver, or accountable role is visible.
- [ ] The output does not imply sign-off has already happened.
- [ ] The approval boundary is explicit when approval is partial or limited.

If the owner is unknown, the output may be **review_ready** but not **approved_for_use**.

### 8. Memory gate

- [ ] Nothing inferred, defaulted, unsupported, or risky is being treated as confirmed reusable memory.
- [ ] Any reusable defaults are clearly marked as candidates unless confirmed.
- [ ] Durable memory promotion is held back when approval is incomplete.
- [ ] Approval status and memory status are not being conflated.

An output can be approved for use in one context without being safe for durable Memory. Keep those decisions separate.

### 9. Packaging gate

- [ ] The output clearly states its readiness level.
- [ ] The output distinguishes planning notes from approved content.
- [ ] Open blockers, review dependencies, and approval limits are visible.
- [ ] The final recommendation does not overstate readiness.

If the packaging could mislead a downstream user into thinking approval already happened, use **blocked_from_approval** until the readiness framing is corrected.

## Approval decision rules

### Use planning_ready when

- the work is useful for scoping, intake, routing, or next-step planning
- approvals are not yet the point of the current artifact
- evidence, claim review, or source approval is still incomplete
- the output clearly labels open uncertainty

### Use review_ready when

- the output is structured enough for reviewer evaluation
- major gaps are surfaced clearly
- the next action is formal review, not direct use
- the material is useful, but sign-off is still pending

### Use approval_ready when

- the output is packaged properly for an approval decision
- the relevant sources and review context are visible
- any remaining issues are small enough that the named owner can reasonably decide
- the artifact is ready to be accepted, rejected, or approved with limits

### Use approved_for_use when

- approval has actually happened
- the approved scope is clear
- no hidden claim, source, exclusion, or readiness blockers remain within that scope
- the output is safe to use in the intended context

### Use blocked_from_approval when

- a material dependency is still missing
- the wrong source authority is being used
- risky or unsupported claims remain unresolved
- exclusions or sensitive-content rules are being crossed
- the output would mislead others about readiness or sign-off

---

## Approval-with-limits rule

Approval may be valid only within limits.

Examples:

- approved for internal planning only
- approved for draft stakeholder review, not publication
- approved for page structure, but not for proof claims
- approved for messaging direction, but not for legal wording
- approved for current-run use, but not for durable Memory

When limits exist:

- say them explicitly
- do not collapse partial approval into full approval
- keep the boundary attached to the output

## Example approval summaries

### Example: planning_ready

- Intake structure is strong enough to move forward.
- Questionnaire-derived defaults are still provisional.
- Approved sources and claim evidence still need confirmation before this becomes review-ready for publication decisions.

### Example: review_ready

- The draft is ready for stakeholder review.
- Claim-sensitive FAQ items are flagged correctly.
- Source approval is still pending for two trust statements, so this is not yet approved for use.

### Example: approval_ready

- The page pack is ready for decision by the content lead.
- Source authority, exclusions, and claim review notes are attached.
- Approval should explicitly confirm whether the proof statements are in or out.

### Example: approved_for_use

- Approved for internal use as the current page brief and approved source-of-truth summary.
- Not approved for public-facing proof claims outside the reviewed scope.
- Safe to reuse for drafting within the stated boundaries.

### Example: blocked_from_approval

- The output currently implies approved proof and compliance certainty without the required evidence or reviewer sign-off.
- Do not route this onward as approval-ready.
- Narrow the wording and complete review first.
