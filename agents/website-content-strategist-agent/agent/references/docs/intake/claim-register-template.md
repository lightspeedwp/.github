## Purpose

This file is the current-state working template for capturing, reviewing, approving, rejecting, and rewriting claims that arise during intake, drafting, FAQ planning, stats intake, governance review, and public-facing content creation.

Use it when a workflow includes:

- medical, health, legal, financial, safety, or regulated content
- pricing, ROI, conversion, ranking, performance, or outcome claims
- testimonials, case studies, statistics, guarantees, comparisons, or awards
- externally published factual claims
- questionnaire-derived defaults that imply compliance, quality, suitability, or safety
- AI-generated recommendations that could affect decisions or public wording

Unsupported or high-risk claims must not be used in final public-facing output unless reviewed.

---

## Core rules

- A visible statement can still be an unsupported claim.
- Questionnaire material is not claim approval.
- Public URLs are not claim approval.
- Screenshots are not claim approval.
- Draft language is not claim approval.
- If a claim is comparative, quantified, regulated, guaranteed, performance-based, or trust-sensitive, assume review is needed unless approved evidence clearly says otherwise.
- If a claim cannot be supported safely, rewrite it into a lower-risk alternative or hold it back.
- Keep claim review separate from general drafting so public-facing outputs do not silently inherit unsupported proof.

---

## Required fields

Use this schema for each claim entry.

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Claim | The exact or near-exact statement needing review |
| Claim type | Category of claim |
| Source | Immediate source where the claim appeared |
| Questionnaire source | Questionnaire file name when applicable |
| Evidence status | `confirmed`, `inferred`, `defaulted`, `missing`, or `unsupported` |
| Risk level | `low`, `medium`, or `high` |
| Approved for use | `yes`, `no`, or `needs_review` |
| Reviewer | Person, role, or review owner |
| Notes | Evidence gap, rewrite direction, limits, or escalation detail |

```yaml
claim_register:
```

## Evidence-status guidance

| Evidence status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| confirmed | Explicitly supported by an approved source or confirmed by the user/owner. |
| inferred | Likely true, but not yet directly supported by approved evidence. |
| defaulted | Introduced as a smart default or working assumption rather than supported proof. |
| missing | A claim exists, but evidence has not yet been supplied. |
| unsupported | The available evidence does not support the claim, or the claim should not be used. |

---

## Risk-level guidance

### Low

Use when the claim is:

- operationally modest
- clearly supported
- not regulated
- unlikely to mislead if phrased narrowly

### Medium

Use when the claim is:

- public-facing and trust-relevant
- somewhat comparative or performance-oriented
- partially supported but still needs validation or owner review

### High

Use when the claim is:

- regulated, legal, financial, medical, safety-related, or privacy-sensitive
- a guarantee or strong outcome promise
- a quantified or comparative statement without strong approved evidence
- likely to create real trust, compliance, or reputational risk if wrong

---

## Approval guidance

### Approved for use: yes

Use only when:

- the evidence is strong enough
- the source is approved
- the wording stays within approved scope
- any required reviewer has cleared the claim

### Approved for use: needs_review

Use when:

- the claim may be valid but still needs evidence or reviewer sign-off
- the claim comes from a public source or questionnaire but is not yet approved
- the claim needs narrowing, qualification, or source confirmation before use

### Approved for use: no

Use when:

- the claim is unsupported
- the claim is too risky for the workflow
- the claim would mislead, overpromise, or imply unverified proof
- the claim should be replaced with safer wording

```md
- Claim type: <type>
- Source: <source label or location>
- Questionnaire source: <file name or none>
- Evidence status: <confirmed | inferred | defaulted | missing | unsupported>
- Risk level: <low | medium | high>
- Approved for use: <yes | no | needs_review>
- Reviewer: <person / role / unknown>
- Notes:
  - <evidence summary>
  - <rewrite direction or review dependency>
```

---

## Review workflow

For each claim:

1. capture the exact wording or closest working wording
2. identify the source and whether that source is approved
3. classify the evidence status
4. assign a risk level
5. decide whether the claim is approved, blocked, or pending review
6. record the review owner when one exists
7. note the safer rewrite if the original wording should not be used

---

## Rewrite rules for unsafe claims

If a claim is unsupported or too risky:

- remove exact numbers if the number is not approved
- remove rankings or superlatives if they are not evidenced
- remove guarantees and replace with narrower process language
- remove implied compliance certainty if formal review is missing
- replace broad outcome promises with more cautious benefit framing
- keep the useful persuasive intent only if it can be expressed safely

Examples:

- instead of "Trusted by 500+ clients" use "Trusted by clients across a range of projects" if the number is unapproved
- instead of "Guaranteed results" use "Built to support consistent improvement" if no guarantee is approved
- instead of "Fully compliant" use "Designed with compliance requirements in mind" if formal legal approval is missing

---

## When to require a claim register

Require or recommend a claim register when:

- the page, FAQ, policy, chatbot, or review output includes factual public-facing claims
- stats or proof blocks are present
- questionnaire defaults imply trust, quality, authority, suitability, or safety
- FAQ answers include guarantees, comparisons, rankings, or operational promises
- AI-generated recommendations may influence public wording or decision-making
- policy, governance, or trust-page language could be mistaken for legal certainty
