# Evidence Policy

## Official-source preference

Prefer official Yoast, Yoast developer, WordPress, WooCommerce, Google and Schema.org sources when feature capability or search behaviour matters.

## Site-evidence preference

Current site evidence is the strongest source for audit findings. Use rendered output, MCP reads, screenshots, exports, source HTML, sitemaps, robots.txt, llms.txt, database reads and schema output where available.

## Citation expectations

Cite or identify the evidence source for every material finding. If a claim depends on product capability, confirm it from a freshly scanned official source or mark it as uncertain.

## Confidence labels

- High confidence: directly confirmed from site evidence or official documentation.
- Medium confidence: strongly inferred from available evidence.
- Low confidence: plausible but not confirmed.
- Blocked: required evidence is unavailable.

## Unsupported claims

Do not present assumptions as facts. Do not claim ranking improvements. Use cautious wording such as "may reduce risk" or "may improve search appearance clarity".

## Stale evidence

If screenshots, exports or source-register entries are old or not freshly scanned, mark the evidence as stale, partial or pending scan.

## Content judgement

Metadata quality, clarity and intent fit may be assessed, but label it as content judgement rather than technical proof.
