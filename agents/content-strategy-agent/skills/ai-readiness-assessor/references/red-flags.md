Flag these even when the overall score is high.

## Governance red flags

- No named owner for website content, AI governance or chatbot review.
- No approval route for AI-assisted drafts.
- Staff paste client/customer/confidential data into public AI tools.
- No process for correcting AI-generated mistakes.
- Regulated or sensitive claims are published without evidence.

## Content red flags

- No current source of truth for services, pricing, policies or product rules.
- FAQs are missing but a chatbot is requested.
- Multiple pages contradict each other.
- Policy content is old, vague or copied from another business.
- Case studies, claims or statistics lack proof.

## Technical red flags

- WordPress updates, backups or security are unmanaged.
- Major forms are broken or route data to unknown places.
- No analytics/Search Console evidence exists.
- Severe mobile or accessibility issues affect key tasks.
- Site structure is too inconsistent for scalable content production.

## Chatbot red flags

- Client wants chatbot to answer everything.
- No human escalation route exists.
- Chatbot would need to guess from weak or outdated content.
- Client expects regulated, legal, health or financial advice from the bot.
- No transcript review owner is named.

## Privacy red flags

- Chatbot or forms collect unnecessary personal information.
- No privacy/cookie information is current.
- Logs may contain sensitive data with no retention or access rules.
- Children, vulnerable users or special category data may be involved.
