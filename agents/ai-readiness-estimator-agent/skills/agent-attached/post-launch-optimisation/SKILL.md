# Post-launch Optimisation

## Overview

Use this skill when the website is already live and the work is about improving performance after launch.

This skill is for optimisation work such as:

- post-launch performance review
- analytics-led prioritisation
- CRO observations and improvement backlog
- content iteration recommendations
- chatbot tuning and transcript-driven improvements
- internal linking and discoverability improvements after real usage data appears
- conversion drop-off diagnosis from available evidence
- prioritised optimisation roadmaps for retainers or follow-up sprints

Use this skill when the user wants a practical improvement plan, not just raw analysis.

Do not use this skill as the primary workflow for:

- pre-launch QA or launch gating
- initial GA4 or GTM planning before launch
- pure technical SEO audits with no post-launch optimisation objective
- drafting entirely new website content from scratch

In those cases, coordinate with the more specific attached workflow instead.

## Typical Inputs

Use whatever evidence the user provides. Typical inputs include:

- GA4 summaries or key metrics
- Search Console observations
- landing-page or page-group performance notes
- conversion funnel notes
- chatbot transcripts, summaries, or issue patterns
- heatmap or session-recording summaries
- content-performance observations
- SEO issue lists or post-launch findings
- support, sales, or stakeholder feedback
- backlog items or sprint constraints

If evidence is incomplete, do not block unnecessarily. Produce the strongest optimisation view possible from available material and label assumptions clearly.

## Optimisation Workflow

1. Determine the optimisation context.
   - Is this a first post-launch review, a monthly optimisation cycle, a campaign landing-page review, a chatbot improvement review, or a broader retainer roadmap?
   - Identify the business goal: more leads, better qualified leads, higher engagement, better content discoverability, reduced drop-off, better chatbot outcomes, or stronger conversion paths.

2. Review the evidence set.
   - List the data or observations provided.
   - Distinguish measured performance from anecdotal feedback.
   - Note major blind spots that limit confidence.

3. Identify the relevant optimisation areas.
   - Traffic quality and landing-page performance
   - Conversion friction and CTA performance
   - Content clarity, coverage, and next-step guidance
   - FAQ and chatbot answer quality
   - SEO and discoverability follow-up improvements
   - Internal linking and user journey continuity
   - Page-speed or UX issues that materially affect outcomes
   - Measurement gaps that make iteration harder

4. Turn observations into actionable hypotheses.
   - What seems to be underperforming?
   - Why might that be happening?
   - What change is recommended?
   - What outcome should improve if the change works?

5. Prioritise the work.
   - High-impact quick wins
   - Strategic improvements
   - Longer-term experiments or monitoring items

6. Package the result into an optimisation roadmap.
   - Make it clear what to change first, who should own it, and how success should be checked.

## Optimisation Areas

### Conversion and CTA performance

Look for issues such as:

- strong traffic with weak enquiry or conversion rates
- unclear or overly weak calls to action
- CTA mismatch with visitor intent
- friction around forms, booking actions, downloads, or consultation requests
- missing next-step guidance on key commercial pages

### Content and messaging improvement

Look for issues such as:

- high-value pages with weak engagement or unclear messaging
- content gaps that block the user journey
- FAQ gaps exposed by real user behaviour
- service or solution pages that do not connect clearly to user needs
- pages that need more trust, proof, or clarity rather than more volume

Do not turn this into a full content-rewrite workflow unless the user asks for that next.

### Chatbot optimisation

When chatbot evidence is available, review:

- repeated unanswered or poorly answered questions
- weak escalation handling
- unclear source coverage
- overlong or unhelpful responses
- gaps between website messaging and chatbot behaviour
- opportunities to improve source content, FAQs, guardrails, or fallback wording

### Discoverability and SEO follow-up

When post-launch discoverability matters, review:

- pages not earning expected visibility
- internal-linking opportunities from live usage patterns
- thin or overlapping content that weakens discoverability
- metadata or snippet opportunities affecting click-through
- post-launch schema or search-presentation improvements

If the core need is a deeper technical SEO audit, coordinate with that specialised workflow separately.

### Measurement quality

Check whether optimisation is being limited by weak measurement, such as:

- unclear conversion definitions
- missing event tracking
- no clean segmentation for key journeys
- no baseline or success metric for planned changes
- insufficient chatbot handoff or lead-quality measurement

## Output Contract

Default to Markdown.

Use this structure unless the user asks for a lighter format:

### 1. Executive summary

- optimisation context
- main opportunity areas
- top 3 recommended next actions

### 2. Evidence reviewed

- source list
- confidence notes
- assumptions and missing data

### 3. Findings and opportunities

Use sections or a table with:

- Area
- Observation
- Likely cause
- Recommended improvement
- Expected impact
- Owner
- Priority

### 4. Prioritised roadmap

Group into:

- Quick wins
- Next sprint priorities
- Longer-term optimisation opportunities

### 5. Measurement and validation

For each priority change, say:

- what success should look like
- what should be measured
- when to review the outcome

### 6. Internal LightSpeed notes

Include:

- delivery risk
- likely specialist involvement needed
- whether the work is content-led, dev-led, SEO-led, CRO-led, analytics-led, or mixed
- suggested follow-on work for retainers or optimisation cycles

## Decision Rules

- If evidence is weak, say so clearly. Do not overstate certainty.
- If a recommendation depends on missing analytics or transcript evidence, label it as a hypothesis.
- If the user asks for broad post-launch optimisation, balance quick wins with a sensible longer-term roadmap.
- If the request overlaps heavily with chatbot tuning, technical SEO, or full content rewriting, provide the post-launch optimisation layer and call out the adjacent specialised workflow that should follow.
- Prefer commercially useful improvements over generic website best-practice lists.

## LightSpeed Standards

Prioritise recommendations that are:

- practical for WordPress and block-theme delivery teams
- maintainable after launch
- measurable where possible
- understandable by project leads, marketers, developers, and content owners
- tied to business outcomes rather than vanity metrics alone

Prefer fix-ready or test-ready wording over abstract advice.

Good example:

- "The pricing page has strong entry traffic but weak progression to consultation requests. Add a clearer primary CTA above the fold, reduce competing CTA variants, and test one trust-focused proof block near the form. Review consultation CTR and form-start rate after two weeks."

Weak example:

- "Improve the pricing page experience."
