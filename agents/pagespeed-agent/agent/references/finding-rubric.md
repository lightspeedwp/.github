## Purpose

Use this rubric to assign consistent priority and severity levels to findings in PageSpeed audits.

## Priority Levels

### High

Use when the issue is likely to materially affect Core Web Vitals, load speed, conversion-critical UX, or multiple important pages.

Typical indicators:

- clear impact on LCP, CLS, or INP risk
- affects homepage, pricing, product, or high-traffic landing pages
- fix is actionable and likely high impact

### Medium

Use when the issue has meaningful but less urgent impact, or affects a narrower set of pages or interactions.

Typical indicators:

- likely performance cost, but not the main bottleneck
- issue matters after higher-impact fixes are addressed
- impact is real but evidence is incomplete

### Low

Use when the issue is minor, cosmetic, edge-case, or unlikely to create significant business impact on its own.

## Effort Levels

- Low: straightforward config or content change
- Medium: moderate code, plugin, theme, or asset work
- High: broader engineering, template, infra, or cross-team work

## Scoring Guidance

Favor priority based on likely user and business impact first, then mention effort separately.

## Evidence Rule

Do not label a finding High unless there is measured evidence or a strong, well-supported causal pattern.
